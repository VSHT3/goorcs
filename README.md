# Gorcs

A private, desktop-first 2D tower-defense game for friends, developed with AI assistance.

## Current state

Playable host-authoritative two-player defense through **Play**, mirrored **Singleplayer**, free-build **Sandbox**, persistent collection/chests, and the **Orcieary** enemy guide. Every fresh match/replay starts with a mandatory **Knight / Priestess commander draft before tower loadouts**, followed by independently generated commander-linked terrain and host-owned abilities. Seven collectible towers support private loadouts, rolled attributes, rerolls, and progression branches. Sixteen animated enemies include shielded KamikaOrcs and rare bomb-carrying Giant Skeltorcs alongside the existing roster and bosses debuting at rounds **40 / 60 / 100**. First kills of each boss type award tiered run-only Coins/Shards through the shared rarity reel. Permanent stat growth, post-run progression rewards, and spells beyond the two commander abilities remain unimplemented.

The HUD, camera, tower inspection/accounting, and continuous Ballista tracking have local rendered smoke coverage. Commander draft/terrain/ability controls also passed an actual-main-scene native graphical smoke on Apple M3 Compatibility/OpenGL with dummy audio; see [scope and evidence](docs/development.md#commander-draft-terrain-and-abilities). This is not blanket battlefield-art approval, physical audio/performance validation, or separate-network internet proof. All authored odds, stat bounds, prices, production/drop amounts, and new branch balance are provisional, not final creator-approved tuning. See the [content pipeline](docs/content-pipeline.md) for detailed resource mechanics.

The UI pairs bundled **Pixelify Sans** letters with crisper **Silkscreen** pixel numerals, including numbers inside mixed labels, descriptions, and commander-draft text. The commander guess starts empty, has no spinner arrows, and accepts an explicit whole number from 0–100 through Lock guess or Enter. Small interface text stays at least 16px. Both fonts work offline and include their [Pixelify](assets/fonts/OFL.txt) and [Silkscreen](assets/fonts/silkscreen/OFL.txt) licenses.

The supplied **Orcish Drone** track loops during match planning, combat, wave breaks, and the in-match pause/settings menu. It stops on results or leaving the match. Music stays off in title menus, connection setup, Sandbox, both field guides, and collection. Playback is local and does not restart on host snapshots or volume changes.

## Adding game content

Towers and enemies are authored as Godot resources and registered in one explicit content catalog. Follow the [content pipeline](docs/content-pipeline.md) for resource fields, registration, existing attack behaviors, and verification. New identities, stats, and artwork should not require adding ID branches to gameplay code; genuinely new mechanics still require implementation.

## Run

Use the standard Godot edition and the [required engine release](docs/development.md#setup). Import `project.godot` into Godot and press **F5** to run the project.

For terminal launches, use `./tools/run.sh` to refresh imports and script classes before starting, including after pulling changes. See the [development commands](docs/development.md#setup) for engine selection and verification.

The app/window icon reuses the title-screen orc face, with native Windows/macOS icon files included. Discord Rich Presence shows activity, match mode/round/phase, a per-match elapsed timer, and multiplayer occupancy (1/2 or 2/2), without sharing private invites or loadouts. **Settings → Discord Rich Presence** controls sharing and remembers the choice. See [Discord setup and limits](docs/development.md#discord-rich-presence).

The centered main-menu wordmark uses a green pixel-orc O with pointed ears, heavy brows, a broad jaw, and upward tusks. Its neutral face matches the letters' top and bottom edges, with pixel-snapped rows and matching pointer hit detection. On first load, the logo assembles in crisp pixel blocks, then the orc braces, squeezes its eyes shut, and opens its jaw with a short recorded roar. The tusks descend with the jaw; the face recovers before the finishing light sweep. Buttons work throughout, and neither the entrance nor its sound replays on menu return; occasional blinking/tiny idle lift remain afterward. Leaving early cancels the intro audio. Hover/focus marks stay inside fixed button bounds.

Click the orc O to replay his recorded roar and jaw animation. Every click restarts the current roar rather than stacking voices; clicking during the entrance switches directly to the requested roar. Other letters do not trigger it, and leaving the menu stops it.

**Play** uses a centered menu with a primary Singleplayer action, paired Host/Join choices, and the same button styling/hover effects as the title screen. Connection forms, waiting rooms, real errors, and the X close control stay in the compact column rather than spanning the screen.

Menus support mouse hover and visible keyboard focus; arrows/Tab navigate and Enter activates. Main-menu shortcuts are **P** Play, **S** Sandbox, **B** Orcieary, **T** Towerieary, **I** Itemiery, and **C** Collection. Escape returns or resumes the last activity; text fields/dialogs take priority. The gameplay pointer uses the steel variant from [Gauntlet Cursor by itsmars](https://opengameart.org/content/gauntlet-cursor), based on yd's CC0 artwork, at its original 32×32 size. Clickable UI retains the separate hand pointer; text/resize cursors remain native.

Navigation and dismiss controls use the same compact **X** as Towerieary, including Collection, Orcieary, Sandbox, Play/loadouts, Settings, placement cancellation, and tower/enemy inspection. Escape keeps its existing priority and action; Settings X returns to the pause menu, while lobby X cancels/leaves normally. Collection's tower list scrolls without visible scrollbar chrome so all seven entries remain reachable without pushing the header offscreen.

Main-menu buttons retain their hover/focus highlights without descriptive hover popups.

Menu action activation uses a short, muted wood/metal clack; hovering, moving keyboard focus, and browsing tower identities or upgrade previews stay silent. The clack and title roars both respect **Sound** volume/mute independently of Music. The roar is adapted from rubberduck's [80 CC0 creature SFX](https://opengameart.org/content/80-cc0-creature-sfx); retained source, licensing evidence, and processing details live under `tools/audio_sources/intro_roar/`.

## Commanders, terrain, and abilities

- Each fresh match, including **Play again** and fresh admission after rejoining, begins with a host-hidden random integer from **0 through 100 inclusive**. **67 has a one-in-three chance unless the previous target was 67**; immediately after 67, the host chooses uniformly among the other 100 values. Other non-67 rolls also choose uniformly from those values. This no-repeat memory lasts while the game remains open. Both players lock one immutable integer guess in that range. Only after both lock are the target and both guesses revealed; the closest guess picks first, with one host-owned coin flip for equal distances.
- Players irreversibly pick distinct **Knight / Priestess** commanders in that order. Tower selection and commitment unlock only after both heroes are chosen; both loadout commitments then open board planning. Commanders stay off-board, not controllable units.
- Each board independently receives a randomly located permanent, unbuildable and unpathable landmark: Knight's **Bastion** gives nearby Physical attacking towers **+15% damage**; Priestess's **Sanctuary** gives nearby attacking towers **+15% attack rate**. Both use a **2.5-cell radius** measured between tile centres. Standard boards also receive **nine obstacles** with four distinct looks: split boulders, broken masonry, stumps/logs, and ruined barricades. Compact boards scale the count down to preserve room. Select your own obstacle and use **Clear · 25 Coins** during any planning break **before Ready**; landmarks cannot be cleared. Generation reserves a legal corridor and a two-tile building space.
- During combat, Knight's **Rally** gives all own attacking towers **+30% attack rate for 8 simulation seconds**. Priestess's **Stasis** slows enemies on the selected **Own board / Opponent** board by **40% for 6 simulation seconds**, including arrivals while active. Both are host-owned, cost nothing, and start a **45-combat-simulation-second cooldown on activation**; planning does not advance it. Movement slows use the stronger effect, not multiplicative stacking. Neither ability permanently destroys opposing defenses or damages their base.
- Sandbox retains its existing free-build rules without this draft, terrain, or abilities. These commander values are provisional playtest balance.

## Test solo

- Choose **Play → Singleplayer** without a connection, port, or invite. Complete the commander guessing/picking sequence first; the host owns the opponent's random guess and available-hero pick, so the two heroes differ. Then choose **one to four distinct owned towers** and press **Commit loadout**; empty slots are allowed, an empty deck is not. Your opponent mirrors that deck.
- The initial view shows your own side of the continuous world. Hold **WASD** or **middle-mouse drag** to pan and inspect the opponent's independently generated commander board; use **scroll / pinch** to zoom smoothly around the cursor. **Home** returns to your side and fits the entire current board. Keyboard panning slows down when zoomed in. The HUD stays fixed and opponent-side input remains read-only.
- The opponent mirrors accepted path edits, tower purchases, targeting, upgrades, rerolls, sales, enemy sends, and readiness. Path placement and tower construction prevalidate terrain on **both** boards, so counterpart terrain can reject an otherwise empty own cell without spending or partially applying the action. Each accepted purchase/reroll is rolled once and copied, with the cost charged to both balances. Own obstacle clearing and commander abilities are **not mirrored**; commander terrain/effects and resulting combat can differ. Pressing **Ready** starts both boards.
- The pixel **> / >> / >>>** button cycles **1× / 2× / 3×** simulation speed. The loop-arrow button cycles **off → autoplay → full autoplay**. Autoplay still waits for Ready in initial setup/main rounds and continues ordinary rounds after both boards clear; full autoplay (a play arrow inside the loop) also starts setup and main rounds once both paths are legal. New runs and round-changing debug jumps reset speed; ordinary waves and money-only changes retain it.
- Collection locks, prices, combat, wave breaks, and results use the same rules as multiplayer. The tower/path mirror is a testing opponent, not independent AI; commander differences can produce different outcomes, and simultaneous defeat still uses the existing host coin flip.
- **Esc or the cogwheel** opens **Resume / Settings / Quit** without ending the run. Solo simulation pauses; Resume continues it. Settings saves independent Music and Sound volume. Combat now has distinct bow/ballista, Rune/explosion, hit/death/bone, portal/crystal, and accepted purchase/upgrade/rejection effects. Overlap limits and distance fade keep hordes readable. Escape first cancels placement/inspection, backs out of Settings, or resumes. Quit returns to the main menu without changing Sandbox, collection, or lifetime records.
- The small **#** button immediately left of the menu cogwheel opens **Round / Money / Shards / Apply** in solo only; multiplayer matches have no way to set them. Changing the round returns to planning before that round without clearing your build or granting rewards; same-round Coin/Shard edits keep combat and committed readiness intact. It sets both mirrored Coin and Shard balances. Earlier rounds retain already-grown ground.

## Play with a friend

- Choose **Play → Host** and press **Create room**. On the same Wi-Fi/LAN, leave the shown Host IP as is: the invite carries every network address of your computer and your friend's game finds the one that works. Type a different address only for a shared VPN or a forwarded internet IP. Allow Gorcs through your firewall if asked (Windows: **Private networks**).
- Use **Copy invite code** and share the complete code privately. Your friend chooses **Play → Join**, pastes it, and presses **Connect** or Enter.
- After admission, both players complete the mandatory secret-guess, ordered distinct-hero draft above. Only then do they choose loadouts together, privately, before board planning. Equip one to four distinct owned identities, then **Commit loadout**. Commitment locks your choices for that run; only the friend's choosing/committed status is shared, not their deck list. Both must commit before boards open. Discover opposing choices by scouting placed towers.
- Planning opens in one continuous world: host on the left, guest on the right, initially focused on your own side. **WASD** or **middle-mouse drag** pans and **scroll / pinch** zooms within bounds around the cursor. **Home** returns from scouting and fits your current board, including map growth. The same camera controls work in Sandbox. Only your own board accepts edits, wherever the camera is looking. Match paths are separate from Sandbox and are not saved.
- Round, adjacent icon-backed Coin/Shard balances, the blue crystal-health gauge, menu cogwheel, and tower controls stay in a fixed HUD over a separate world viewport. Bases start at **100 HP**. A continuous cyan-blue fill sits in a dark navy track beside a pale current-HP value; health does not switch to gold or green. Planning shows tile budget and opponent readiness; alive/mirror counters are absent. Management/notices never resize the world. HUD controls, typing, popups, modals, and tower drags take priority over camera gestures; scrolling HUD panels never zooms the map.
- The tower palette offers your admitted loadout with numeric Coin prices; the host rejects purchases outside that frozen deck. Click a tower then empty non-path ground, or drag it onto your board. **Hold Shift while placing or dropping to keep building the same tower**; release Shift for a normal placement that opens inspection. Repeat placement ends when the tower becomes unaffordable. Right-click or Escape cancels placement. Clicking an owned tower opens accepted stats, rarity/attributes, targeting where applicable, available upgrades/branches, both rerolls, and Sell. Purchased upgrade controls disappear. **Sell takes two presses:** the first turns it into **Confirm · +refund** for 3 seconds, ignoring a double-click; inspecting another tower or closing inspection cancels it. **Backspace** follows the same two-press rule; an accepted sale plays a cash cue respecting Sound volume/mute. Sandbox sells free towers in one press.
- While dragging a tower, a translucent battlefield preview snaps to the hovered owned tile and shows its baseline range as highlighted cells. Invalid ground is marked red. Starting a drag closes tower/enemy inspection so the panel cannot obstruct placement; Escape cancels the drag without opening Pause or leaving Sandbox. Previewing never purchases a tower or rolls traits.
- **1–4** selects the corresponding visible tower-palette slot; the same numpad keys work (**1–6** in Sandbox). Slots remain stable when a tower is unaffordable. Typing in fields, open menus/dialogs, pausing, and active drags take priority over building shortcuts. Tower-palette number hints and explanatory shortcut prose are not displayed.
- Inspect an owned tower and press **B** to select that tower's base type for placement; there is no visible Build another button. Selection spends nothing; the next accepted purchase has its normal price and a fresh roll, without copying the inspected tower's upgrades. Funds, equipped loadout, and committed Ready still apply.
- Tower inspection pins the rarity-prefixed title and compact X while stats, the tower-wide targeting selector, and purchase controls scroll. A stunned, Soulfire-withered or Nullify-silenced tower also pins a small chip per effect under its title (**Stunned 9.5s**, **Withered -50% · 6.0s**, **Silenced 8.0s**), drawn with the same star, green flame and crossed sigil as its battlefield mark. Available upgrade/evolution choices sit side by side above Sell; when a choice's longest word would not fit (four War Drums branches, narrow Sandbox panels) they wrap into balanced rows instead of splitting words. Sell remains the final action. Each rarity has a distinct static title color, without animation; received support is labeled **Buff**.
- The host owns each tower's cumulative **Kills**, **Damage dealt**, **Coins generated**, and **Indirect damage**, shown where relevant and retained through upgrades, evolutions, rerolls, and waves. Direct attacker damage and Bounty Board amplification partition actual HP removed, excluding overkill and double counting. Generated Coins count Mine/Mint production plus Board/Tribute bonus Coins, not ordinary kill bounty. These counters do not change damage, payout, or rounding rules.
- Build the initial path before round 1. Later, paths change only during **main-round planning before rounds 10, 20, 30…**. Clear path appears only in those main rounds; right after a Clear it becomes **Undo clear**, restoring the removed tiles (except any now under a tower) until you draw again or the break ends. With no tower selected, left/right drag edits a permitted path; otherwise board clicks inspect. Combat and normal-round paths remain locked.
- **Ready commits your planning actions:** no Unready, path edits, purchases, upgrades, rerolls, selling, targeting, or sends until combat starts. Both Ready starts sooner. On normal rounds, the first Ready starts a **20-real-second** host countdown and the round starts automatically at expiry; 2× speed does not shorten it. Initial setup and main rounds are untimed, requiring both Ready.
- Before rounds **20, 30, 40…**, both maps gain a row above, a row below, and a column on the outer/crystal side, plus **+6 path tiles**. The portal-facing edge never grows. **Portal and crystal positions do not move**, nor do existing towers/paths; the new outer column lies beyond the crystal. The camera frames the enlarged owned board once, without expansion highlights; subsequent snapshots preserve your view. Values remain provisional.
- The pixel **> / >> / >>>** and loop-arrow buttons request speed/autoplay. In multiplayer, clicking speed votes **2×**, clicking again votes **3×**, and a third click withdraws. Each speed needs both players' consent: if one picks 3× and the other 2×, the match runs at 2× until both pick 3×. Either withdrawal stops the shared effect without erasing the other request. Gold is pending; green is agreed. Autoplay pauses for untimed initial/main strategy, keeps consent, and resumes ordinary rounds after both Ready; full autoplay needs both players' full-autoplay consent and skips that pause. Results never restart.
- Arrow and Ballista initially hit one target. Arrow's later special adds a second arrow using the same tower-wide targeting policy; archers prefer distinct enemies but share targets when too few are in range. Ballista instead chooses a straight-through **Pierce 2** bolt or a redirected **Bounce 1** bolt. Tower range is a circle around the tower's tile (distance between tile centres), so fractional range bonuses add only the tiles they actually reach and a small rolled bonus never adds a whole ring. Unevolved Rune (1.5 range) hits every enemy in its surrounding 3×3 tile neighborhood with 2-damage pulses (+1 from its damage upgrade), with a 0.7-simulation-second base interval and no damage between pulses. Each pulse retains the brief ground shimmer and local hit sparks. Its later evolution chooses continuous Boulders damage or heavier Onyx Ring pulses. Selected range is quietly highlighted tiles, not an attack ring. Targeting uses one small, unboxed **< current option >** selector per tower without explanatory hover popups in matches and Sandbox, wrapping through **First → Last → Closest → Strongest** in either direction. First prioritizes greatest route progress; **Last** prioritizes least route progress, closest to the start; Closest means nearest to the tower; Strongest means highest current HP. The host owns this shared policy and preserves it across upgrades, evolutions, and reloads.
- Enemies alternate between equally short branches instead of all taking one side. The host assigns each enemy its shortest route at spawn, using a per-board round-robin choice at each fork; regular and sent enemies share that rotation, reset each wave. Longer detours stay unused. Visuals, facing, and selected-enemy highlights follow that individual route smoothly through corners. Connected pavement still has no route-line overlay.
- Every enemy has articulated walking and an authored death collapse. The basic Orc uses a creator-approved GameLab design with locally articulated walk/death frames, exercised in the running Sandbox. Chiefs raise a palm to heal and NecOrcmancers raise their staff to cast. Living Orc deaths leave a small blood splatter; Skeltorcs collapse into bloodless bones. Bodies/effects fade and never remain targetable; gameplay death bombs are separate host-owned state, not corpse effects.
- The host creates the same weighted group roster/timetable for both boards. Orcling events roll 2–4 members in close succession, then wait the ordinary cooldown. Spawn cadence still tightens linearly with rounds; grouping, health/speed, weight/share caps, and reward scaling are separate rules.
- Enemy movement now grows more slowly over time: each extra **10% of original speed** takes **5, 10, 15, 20… completed rounds**, with gradual increases between milestones and no compounding. Existing type speed ratios remain intact. See [movement progression](docs/enemy-roster.md#movement-progression).
- Enemy HP grows by **+1 base HP per completed round**, down from +2, before each type's multiplier and rounding. Starting HP is unchanged. Normal NecOrcmancers also move slightly slower; see [current balance](docs/enemy-roster.md).
- Every boss alive on your own board gets a health row at the bottom left, above the tower palette: portrait, name and accepted HP. Recent damage leaves a pale trail that drains after a short hold; enraged bosses turn orange-red, an immune channel violet, a hit shield or Barrier adds a gold rim, and Dreadmaw's enrage threshold is notched. A slain boss drains to empty before its row fades; a leaked one just fades. Up to three rows show, in spawn order. Clicking a row inspects that boss. Sandbox shows the same rows.
- Big moments get short beats that never block play: a boss arriving on your board shows its portrait and name under the header, and a cleared round shows the Coins and Shards it paid. A leak shakes and flashes the crystal with the HP lost and pops the loss beside the health gauge; red corners pulse around a crystal an enemy is about to reach. Elite and boss kills burst and pop their bounty. See [match feedback](docs/development.md#match-feedback-beats).
- Click an enemy on either board to inspect that individual. Corner highlights follow it along the path; a fixed right-side overlay shows its short description, health bar, current HP, speed in cells/second, and damage to the base beside heart, boot, and sword icons. Stats update from accepted live enemy state. Click another enemy to switch; empty ground, ×, right-click, or Escape dismisses it. Death, reaching the base, results, and leaving also clear inspection. Tower placement takes priority while a tower is selected for building.
- Each entrance is an animated red stone-ring portal and each base a blue crystal on a stone pedestal. Both use creator-approved 128×128 GameLab stills with free locally authored idle loops: interior portal swirl and crystal-facet shimmer, with fixed masonry. Their cosmetic loops are shared by matches and Sandbox and do not accelerate at 2× simulation speed. The replacement artwork and hide/resume playback were inspected in the running Sandbox. Camera scouting never grants additional usable ground or path budget.
- Match artwork uses per-resource opaque height and foot/base anchors with foot-height depth sorting. Towers sit lower, with their front foundation edges near the bottom of their tiles, without added oval shadows or selection circles; the Rune's orbiting boulders also cast no added shadows. Portal and crystal foundations likewise have no added ground-shadow ovals; units retain contact shadows. Towers are substantial structures beside enemies. The playable field has broad restrained grass/soil variation, an external earth edge, and clear contrast against darker surrounding scenery and the central ravine. Continuous orderly stonework defines paths; decoration implies no new blocked cells or footprints. Selection and placement legality are contextual, selected towers retain tile-range highlights, idle combat has no hover square, and only damaged enemies show health bars. Camera fitting reserves resource-derived headroom so top-row towers clear the fixed HUD.
- A destroyed base ends the run. If both bases reach zero in the same host simulation tick, one host-owned coin flip chooses the winner. Each peer gets a scrollable Victory/Defeat recap: outcome/fatal enemy, reached/cleared rounds, combat time, own/opponent totals, Coin/Shard income and spending, tower damage rankings including sold towers, per-enemy kills/sends/leaks/HP lost, and accepted boss loot. Header and actions stay fixed while you scroll. **View battlefield / Esc** dismisses it without unlocking gameplay; click owned towers to inspect final stats with all mutations locked. Escape first clears a selected tower, then reopens results; **Results** or the cog also reopens them. **Main menu** leaves normally. Solo **Play again** starts a fresh commander draft before loadout selection and fresh boards. See [results and playback](docs/match-results.md).
- Coins fund towers, role-specific upgrades, specials/evolutions, and attribute rerolls; kills and cleared waves earn Coins. The original damage/speed upgrades lead from **I → II → III**, then fixed-price specials/evolutions and eligible follow-ups. Arrow gains **Second Archer**, then chooses **Four Archers**, **Fire Arrows**, or **Bomb Arrows**. Ballista permanently chooses **Pierce 2** or **Bounce 1**. Rune permanently chooses continuous **Boulders** or pulsed **Onyx Ring**; only Boulders can then buy **Freeze Runes**, which immediately slows ice-susceptible enemies by 20% on positive exposure, even before fractional damage reaches a whole HP. The host refreshes a nonstacking timer of `2 / (1 + max_hp / 100)` simulation seconds while exposed: higher maximum HP means faster thawing, not a weaker slow. Leaving range or selling the tower leaves the existing slow until expiry; ice-immune enemies ignore it. Rolled modifiers survive these purchases and combine with branch mechanics. Selling refunds part of the actual investment. Detailed authored mechanics and provisional values live in the [content pipeline](docs/content-pipeline.md) and `resources/combat/towers/`.

(5000)**, the Super Ultimate Mega Giga Giga Magic, splits it into five bolts at 95% dropping 3 points per bounce. Magic bolts curve between victims with a glowing violet trail, split bolts fan out, and the tower shows a charged bolt tip, rune ring and orbiting motes. All Ballista bolts are drawn as heavier bolts that grow out of the muzzle. Multipliers compound; prices and values are provisional.

All Drums upgrade/evolution/follow-up prices are 50% higher, rounded up; the base purchase stays 110. Only Horn grows its own aura radius or grants range; shipped Drums cannot roll range. The strongest support bonus per stat wins across all support groups, while different stats coexist; support never extends another support aura. Pure branches do not inherit unrelated base purchases. Only Storm Sigil still gains Rune range (+0.5, +1, +1).
- **Orcapult (120)** is a siege mangonel that grabs the **nearest** throwable enemies in range every 6 s and hurls them, visibly, onto the **opponent's board**: the arm whips forward, the enemy tumbles over the gap and crashes down at the opponent's portal in a dust burst (back to your own portal in Sandbox). Thrown enemies keep their HP and wear dizzy stars; they can never be thrown again, so two Orcapults cannot juggle one forever. Bosses are never lifted, and the arm's **lift** limits weight (base: Grunt-sized). The bucket slams enemies next to each grabbed one (the tower's damage). The round does not end while an enemy is airborne. **Slam/Speed → Double Scoop** (two per throw) then choose **Siege Arm** (lifts Brutes; Iron Counterweight → Ogre Crew → Warlord's Trebuchet hurls Chiefs/MossOrcs/NecOrcmancers) or **Long Toss** (lands enemies down the opponent's path, faster; Greased Sling → Rude Awakening → Over the Wall). Art is drawn by `tools/author_orcapult.py`: a stone-slab base plus a separately rotated arm layer per branch. Provisional balance was tuned with head-to-head simulations against arrow-only spending.
- **Orcapult occupies two horizontal tiles** at every stage. Placement needs both tiles free, including on the mirrored solo board; either tile opens the same tower inspection. Its wide chassis and frame are authored on a native 192×128 canvas, with normally proportioned animated arms rather than stretched textures. Collection, battlefield, and guide share the new design. Native rendered coverage includes placement, second-cell inspection, a purchased finale, and every guide stage; see [verification evidence](docs/development.md#pixel-typography).
- **Gold Mine** pays on round clear. **Royal Mint** retains that income and adds combat-simulation-time production, never interest. **Bounty Board** replaces production with one exclusive, persistent highest-maximum-HP enemy mark that increases damage taken and normal kill Coins. It then permanently chooses stronger marking (**Greater Bounty**) or a killing-tower-location income aura (**Tribute Office**); the aura clears its active mark. Strongest duplicate income auras apply, and another Board's mark can combine with the aura. These follow-up names and balance are provisional. All four upgrades use approved reference-free GameLab stills; [sources and framing](archive_assets/README.md#approved-gold-mine-upgrades) are archived, and every stage is covered by the rendered shared-portrait checks.
- Only **Orclings** can be sent in rounds 1–5. **Orc / Runner / Brute / Chief / NecOrcmancer** sends unlock at rounds **6 / 12 / 22 / 34 / 46**. Orders use an independent paced FIFO throughout combat; planning orders wait for wave start. The send list runs in unlock order; locked types show their round (**Brute · round 22**). At most **20 orders** can wait off-screen for one board at a time (a group counts once); a full queue disables **Send** until orders arrive. Heavier orcs leave a longer gap before the next order: **+5% per wave-weight point above 5** (Grunt/Runner/Brute unchanged, HeavyDutyOrc ×1.25, Chief ×1.35, bosses several times longer). One Orcling order buys its whole rolled group. Rejected orders spend/roll nothing; pending sends and living summons must clear before a wave ends. Healing Chiefs and armored HeavyDutyOrcs were by far the strongest pressure per Coin in balance-bot tests, so they cost **130** and **110** Coins; KamikaOrc sends cost **200**.
- Regular waves introduce **Runner at 6**, **Brute at 12**, **Chief at 22**, and **NecOrcmancer at 34**, with count/share limits. Chiefs periodically heal nearby allies; NecOrcmancers raise bounded Skeltorcs on their remaining captured route. Skeltorcs are summon-only outside Sandbox; their stable `skeleorc` save ID preserves existing records. See [enemy balance, abilities, and verification](docs/enemy-roster.md).
- **Scrapguard / CrateOrc / RageOrc / MossOrc** enter regular waves at **9 / 18 / 28 / 52**, with paid sends at **16 / 28 / 40 / 66**. They respectively resist physical damage, release two Orclings on death, permanently accelerate at half HP, and periodically regenerate. Full mechanics and provisional tuning: [enemy roster](docs/enemy-roster.md#varied-regular-enemies).
- **Elements:** every attacking tower deals **Physical, Fire, Ice, Lightning or Magic** (explosions count as Fire), shown in the HUD and Towerieary. **Ember / Frost / Storm / Arcane Orcs** (rounds 10 / 13 / 15 / 17) are each immune to one element, the iron-plated **EleMetal Orc** (round 20) is immune to Physical but takes 50% more Lightning, and the elite **Omega Elemental Orc** (round 32) can only be hurt by Physical damage. Towers skip enemies they cannot hurt. Details: [enemy roster](docs/enemy-roster.md#elements-and-elemental-orcs).
- **ShaOrcs** (round 24) raise their staffs every 6 s to heal nearby orcs for 15% of their maximum HP and restore their own one-hit shield; nearby ShaOrcs build **OrcShakra** and cast up to 40% faster (+20 / 30 / 35 / 37.5%). The elite **Greater ShaOrc** (round 60, 400-Coin send) wards every other non-boss orc within 3.5 cells every 12 s, making them immune to all damage for 10 s, until it dies. Details: [enemy roster](docs/enemy-roster.md#shaorcs).
- **HeavyDutyOrc** (round 30) is the slowest regular orc: 9× HP in industrial plate (Physical ×0.5, everything else ×0.9) that leaks for 6.
- **Wraithorc** (elite, round 120) phases every 3 s; while ethereal it is translucent and Physical and Fire pass straight through it.
- **Grand Mage Orcafus** (boss, round 140) glides between spells: an immune Nullify channel that silences every Magic tower for 8 s, a Fireball that stuns towers in an area, a Portal that pulls the rearmost 10 orcs forward, a Haste aura, and a Barrier (10-hit shield + 20% heal) below half HP, at most twice. Immune to Fire, Ice and Magic, he regenerates slowly and, when slain, stuns your most valuable tower for 10 s before crumbling to dust. [Details](docs/enemy-roster.md#grand-mage-orcafus-round-140).
- **Dreadmaw, the Bone Wyrm** (boss, round 200) breathes Soulfire that halves the damage of your densest towers for 8 s, raises recently fallen orcs as Skeltorcs, enrages at half HP and spills 8 Skeltorcs on death. Bone resists Physical, Fire burns it, Ice does nothing. [Details](docs/enemy-roster.md#dreadmaw-the-bone-wyrm-round-200).
- **Sandbox:** 8× speed, rounds up to 9999, the enemy picker grouped into Basic / Elite / Boss with portraits, **E** spawns the selected enemy, and **Max upgrades** (or **M**) fully upgrades the selected tower for free.

- From **round 151**, every new **Skeltorc** birth has one host-owned **1%** chance to become a **Giant Skeltorc**: **50× the ordinary rounded HP**, **20% slower**, the same fire/ice immunities, zero Coin bounty and unchanged Shard drop. Its backpack bomb falls on its death tile, lights a **two-second simulation fuse**, then stuns living enemies and towers within **two tiles** for **three seconds**, without HP damage or extra Coins. Bombs stay on the defending board and hold wave clearance until resolved; leaks and transfers never drop one.

Continuous weapons strip charges at accepted attack cadence rather than physics-frame rate. Real death immediately stuns nearby towers within **one tile** for **three seconds**; crystal leaks do not. Its provisional paid send unlock is round 100.

It consumes **40 weight** and summons ordinary **NecOrcmancers**, which retain their own Skeltorc rituals. It is also available in Sandbox and Orcieary.
- **Trollorc**, guaranteed at **round 60**, is enormous and extremely slow, with **1.5× nonphysical damage vulnerability**, collateral stomps, and nearest-tower **30-second** club stuns. **Orc king war orchine**, guaranteed at **round 100**, follows the painted path on wheels, periodically jumps **2–5 path tiles** with leg deployment/crouch/landing, fires at **two distinct random towers**, and releases larger infantry batches. Fire deals **1.2×** damage to it; lightning deals **0.9×**. Stun suspends every tower role, including support and mine effects. All three boss types can share a late-game wave. [Boss rules](docs/enemy-roster.md#late-game-bosses) own exact values.
- Late-game **boss sends** spend a large Coin price and permanently **reduce recurring income**, rather than increasing it. Both balances must cover the entire cost; rejected orders spend/roll nothing. They use the normal opponent FIFO without the regular-wave encounter cap. [Unlocks, prices and income penalties](docs/enemy-roster.md#rules-and-provisional-balance) remain provisional tuning.
- Your **first kill of each boss type per run** rolls a Broken-to-Mythic bonus of **Coins and Shards**, using the current rarity odds and higher payouts for higher tiers/later rounds. The host credits rewards before the cosmetic reel; repeats, leaks, collateral deaths, and Sandbox grant no bonus. **Click or Enter skips any reel spin to the actual result**, which stays visible; X/Escape closes it. [Reward table and ownership](docs/enemy-roster.md#first-boss-kill-loot).
- The code contains the host's address and a random room credential. There is no online room lookup or NAT traversal service. Use the same LAN, a private network/VPN, or a public host address with UDP forwarding and firewall access.
- **Esc or the cogwheel** opens the local match menu; multiplayer keeps running while it is open. **Quit** in that menu closes your connection and returns to the main menu. If the guest leaves, the host waits for another guest with the same code; rejoining starts with fresh boards. If the host leaves, the room closes and its code expires.
- For network setup, security limits, and two-instance verification, see [direct connection development](docs/development.md#direct-connection-lobby).
- Multiplayer combat uses compressed, baseline-relative updates on a separate reliable channel, with one frame in flight and only the newest unsent state retained. Commands and phase changes stay reliable on their own channel; a slow guest no longer creates a queue of obsolete combat frames. Host targeting, boss event stepping and retained battlefield rendering also avoid repeated whole-horde/whole-map work. Both players need the updated build.

### Tower rolls and rerolls

- Successful paid placement rolls **Broken → Shoddy → Common → Uncommon → Rare → Epic → Legendary → Mythic**, then tower-compatible positive/drawback attributes. You choose the identity, not an unknown summon. Inspection immediately shows the accepted result; there is no pre-purchase preview or reveal choice. Pools prohibit duplicate effects and opposing modifiers to the same stat; lower tiers can have only drawbacks.
- **Reroll rarity** spends run-only **Shards**. Its tiny **1× / 10× / Max** selector updates the button's total price without explanatory UI prose. Every candidate excludes the tower's rarity at the start of the batch using renormalized placement weights and regenerates all attributes. The highest rarity wins; equal tiers keep the first candidate and its attributes. Downgrades remain possible. Pricing sums each successive quadratic reroll cost, and the rarity counter advances by the full batch count; only the attribute-reroll counter resets. **Max** buys every affordable roll without a fixed count cap; mirrored Singleplayer requires both balances to cover their respective costs. Sandbox is free, with **Max = 10**. **Reroll attributes** remains a single Coin-priced roll that keeps rarity and replaces every attribute/value.
- Accepted rarity rerolls play real animated horizontal reels in matches and Sandbox: one for **1×**, **two columns of five** for **10×**, and a centered grid of **single-tile-wide reels for Max**, including Sandbox's ten-result Max. Max uses as many 136-pixel columns as fit, keeping 16-pixel rarity labels and showing typical 24/40-result desktop batches without scrolling. Only batches too large to fit readably need chrome-free scrolling; no results are omitted. Narrow reels land with the complete accepted tile centered and no edge masks over its label. Each candidate has rarity-colored tower cards, a fixed landing center, wind-up, sustained fast spin, continuous braking, and a spring-loaded pointer. The highest-tier first-tie winner is applied before presentation; decorative cards do not represent actual odds. Each reel stages exactly one better Epic/Legendary/Mythic neighbor when a better tier exists; other visible neighbors are no higher than its result and capped at Rare. Left-click/Space/Enter skips to the accepted results, then dismisses on another activation. X/Escape dismisses without undoing the batch. Sound follows the existing volume/mute setting; attribute rerolls remain immediate. See the [historical native rendered batch smoke scope](docs/development.md#rarity-reroll-reel); it predates compact Max, and dummy audio is not physical listening verification.
- The reel's full-screen backdrop is opaque, hiding the updated inspector and battlefield so the accepted rarity cannot be read behind the spin. The host still applies the actual result immediately; skip reveals that same result.
- Runs currently start with **8 Shards**, gain **3 per round clear**, and can gain enemy-specific random drops from all registered enemy types, including sends and summons. Drops belong to the killing board. Zero-normal-bounty Skeltorcs and their Giants cannot generate bonus kill Coins from bounty percentages.
- Rerolls obey normal management/Ready locks. Host validation applies costs/results atomically; rejected purchases/rerolls spend nothing and consume no gameplay roll. Rolls, counters, Coins, and Shards reset with the run, not the collection. Exact profiles, curves, drop resources, support stacking, and bounty payout composition are documented in the [content pipeline](docs/content-pipeline.md).

## Collection and chests

- Choose **Collection**, then **Open free chest**. The chest shakes, opens with light and decorative coins, and reveals the actual rewards together. There is no roulette reel.
- **Arrow is guaranteed**, including on fresh saves and after Reset progress. Existing valid saves retain all unlocks and atomically gain Arrow if missing.
- Collect seven identities: **Arrow tower, Orc ballista, Rune obelisk, Lightning, War Drums, Gold Mine, and Orcapult**. Unlocks provide loadout choices; evolution branches remain within their parent's slot. Arrow/Rune retain accepted PixelLab runtime art; Gold Mine/Lightning use approved GameLab stills and Ballista uses its authorized provisional directional trial. Remaining procedural/branch artwork is not final art approval.
- The orc chest uses cleaned user-supplied PixelLab sprites. Its lid transitions between closed/open poses; light and coin effects remain native Godot effects.
- Provisional reward-count odds are 80% for one, 15% for three, and 5% for five. Draws contain only locked identities and are capped to those remaining. With Arrow already owned, a fresh collection can receive all five other types in one opening.
- Collection/chest icons fit their visible artwork rather than transparent canvas padding. The catalog scrolls independently so the seven-entry roster keeps header and chest actions inside the window.
- **Skip reveal** changes only the animation. Rewards are saved before it starts; leaving and returning cannot reroll or grant them again.
- Unlock all seven identities to complete the collection; further openings are disabled. Free prototype chests do not implement post-run rewards or permanent stat progression.
- **Reset progress** confirms a return to Arrow-only ownership, not an empty collection. It does not alter Sandbox state or enemy lifetime records.
- Collection unlocks and enemy statistics survive restarting. Sandbox layouts last for the running session. See [save ownership and verification](docs/development.md#collection-and-chest-verification).

## Sandbox and field guides

- **Sandbox** replaces Workshop: every tower, upgrade branch, enemy, and chosen round is available without collection locks, Coin costs, tile spending, or defeat.
- **Shift-click / Shift-drop** keeps the selected tower ready for repeated free placement; a normal placement opens that tower's inspection. Right-click/Escape cancels building.
- **1–6** (or numpad) selects the numbered tower in the Sandbox palette without placing or purchasing it. Number fields and modal menus keep keyboard priority.
- **Build another [B]** selects the inspected tower's base identity for a separate free placement, without copying its upgrades or traits.
- Draw paths while stopped, choose a weighted round or an exact enemy/count, pause/resume with **Space**, and test at 1×/2×/4× speed; **F** cycles those speeds. **Restart [R]** clears current enemies and replays the last accepted weighted wave or exact Spawn batch, running immediately with your current build, path, and selected round. Editing the spawn controls alone does not change the recorded test. **Home** recenters and fits the whole Sandbox map after panning, zooming, or resizing.
- Resize the map through width/height controls with confirmation. Menu/Escape pauses and preserves the current build, map, and camera; leaving never changes progression.
- **Orcieary** orders enemies by earliest encounter/send availability and lists their descriptions, portraits, stats, mechanics, and lifetime records. Click a portrait—or focus it and press Enter/Space—to play its action once. Sandbox actions are excluded from records.
- **Towerieary** includes all seven tower identities, every upgrade/branch, role-specific stats, and purchase/total costs. Connected full-size artwork nodes scroll without scrollbar chrome; clicking or arrow-key navigation reveals each selected tier. Nodes and the detail portrait share the battlefield renderer, including all archer weapons/crew, the second storey, evolutions, and Rune orbits. X/Escape closes the guide; reopening restores each tower's selection. Previews use isolated accepted state and never purchase upgrades, unlock towers, or modify a match.
- Towerieary spells out **S.U.M.G.G.M. — Super Ultimate Mega Giga Giga Magic** and its five-way homing-bolt split after the first hit. Each bolt starts with a **95% extra-bounce chance**, decreasing by **3 percentage points** after each successful extra bounce. Magic branch descriptions read their bolt counts and odds from the accepted preview.
- **Itemiery** opens on seven closed rarity doors, with no items in the hall. Click a door—or focus it and press Enter/Space—to watch it open and move forward into its separate shelf room: **Common**, **Uncommon**, **Rare**, **Epic**, **Mythic**, **Legendary**, or **Orcfeccion**. Its **130 items** comprise **20 per rarity from Common through Legendary**, with **10 Orcfeccion**; the original 15 designs remain unchanged. Rooms have four bays across five shelf rows, or two bays across five rows for Orcfeccion, with only matching-rarity items and increasingly rich materials, lighting, and ornament. Unused shelves remain empty, with paging for overflow. Every item has a distinct 32×32 icon and separately authored 96×96 portrait, not an upscale or recolor. Hover/focus outlines a door or item without opening inspection. Item activation shows enlarged detailed art, a brief effect and two or three concise rules bullets at native UI resolution. X/Escape closes inspection and restores its shelf item's focus; the next returns to the focused hall door, and another leaves the guide. X/Escape during entry cancels back to the closed hall. Linked upgrade previews stay nested in the current room and never spend currency. Leaving stops animation/rendering and reopening starts in the closed hall while retaining selection metadata. The guide is read-only; its resource-backed items also have host-authoritative gameplay effects. Normal matches start empty unless their host supplies `CombatRules.starting_items`; Sandbox's **Items** panel edits all 130 owned IDs and exposes real active/bind/feed/upgrade actions. All 130 can coexist. No general item shop, drop, draft, or acquisition path is implemented; intrinsic item effects and their explicit actions still work. See [item runtime and verification](docs/development.md#itemiery-hall-and-rarity-rooms) and the [content pipeline](docs/content-pipeline.md#add-a-guide-item) for authoring provenance.
- Full controls, balance rules, save behavior, and verification: [enemy roster and sandbox](docs/enemy-roster.md).
